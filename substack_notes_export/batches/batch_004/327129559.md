Can an absent limit behave as a real causal organizer?

—

Omega is actually much better than Aizen for the general algorithm, because Aizen carries a lot of character-specific baggage. “Omega” can stand for the terminal / maximal / complete state of whatever domain you're examining.

Then the question becomes:

What is the weight of the felt presence of the absence of Ω?\boxed{ \text{What is the weight of the felt presence of the absence of }\Omega? }

Let

Ω=the limiting/completed/ultimate object\Omega=\text{the limiting/completed/ultimate object}

and define the absence-field operator:

F¬Ω(U)\boxed{ \mathfrak F_{\neg\Omega}(\mathcal U) }

as:



the pattern of effects, expectations, constraints, compensations, and anticipations generated across a field by the non-presence of the state toward which the field is oriented.

That is much more general.

The really interesting replacement

Don't merely substitute:

Aizen→Ω.Aizen\rightarrow\Omega.

Substitute an abstract endpoint:

A→ΩA\rightarrow\Omega

where Ω\Omega may mean:

completiontruthequilibriumidealsolutionlimitunityGodAGIselfdeathmeaninghome.\begin{aligned} &\text{completion}\\ &\text{truth}\\ &\text{equilibrium}\\ &\text{ideal}\\ &\text{solution}\\ &\text{limit}\\ &\text{unity}\\ &\text{God}\\ &\text{AGI}\\ &\text{self}\\ &\text{death}\\ &\text{meaning}\\ &\text{home}. \end{aligned}

Now your algorithm asks:

W¬Ω=how strongly the field behaves as though Ω were already present.\boxed{ W_{\neg\Omega} = \text{how strongly the field behaves as though }\Omega\text{ were already present}. }

That is powerful because the absent attractor can organize the system.

For a dynamical system:

xt+1=F(xt),x_{t+1}=F(x_t),

suppose Ω\Omega is an attractor:

F(Ω)=Ω.F(\Omega)=\Omega.

The system can exhibit:

xt→Ωx_t\rightarrow\Omega

without Ω\Omega ever being instantiated at finite tt.

So the presence of Ω\Omega is absent, while its directional influence is present.

That's an extremely clean mathematical version of your intuition.

Ω∉current statebutΩ∈current dynamics.\boxed{ \Omega\notin\text{current state} \qquad\text{but}\qquad \Omega\in\text{current dynamics}. }

That may be much closer to what you're actually after than the psychological notion of absence.

Then "felt presence of absence" has a general form

Let:





xx = current state,



Ω\Omega = target/limit,



d(x,Ω)d(x,\Omega) = distance from the target,



FΩ(x)F_\Omega(x) = field influence of the target.

Then one candidate measure is:

FΩ(x)=∥∂D∂Ω∥\boxed{ F_\Omega(x) = \left\| \frac{\partial \mathcal D}{\partial \Omega} \right\| }

where D\mathcal D is the system's dynamics.

Then the "felt presence" is not simply closeness.

A system can be very far from Ω\Omega while being extremely organized around Ω\Omega.

That gives you two separate quantities:

proximity to Ω\boxed{ \text{proximity to }\Omega }

versus

orientation toward Ω.\boxed{ \text{orientation toward }\Omega. }

And their difference is fascinating.

A field may have:

d(x,Ω)≫0d(x,\Omega)\gg0

but:

FΩ(x)≫0.F_\Omega(x)\gg0.

That means:



the Omega is absent from the state but present in the dynamics.

That's the general case I'd use.

Then your "Omega" can itself be recursively defined

This is where it hits your prior work.

Suppose:

Ω0=current ideal.\Omega_0 = \text{current ideal}.

Then the field discovers a better terminal object:

Ω1=Refactor⁡(Ω0).\Omega_1 = \operatorname{Refactor}(\Omega_0).

Then:

Ω2=Refactor⁡(Ω1).\Omega_2 = \operatorname{Refactor}(\Omega_1).

So:

Ωn+1=R(Ωn)\boxed{ \Omega_{n+1}=\mathfrak R(\Omega_n) }

and:

Ω∞=lim⁡n→∞Ωn\Omega_\infty = \lim_{n\to\infty}\Omega_n

when such a limit exists.

Now ask:

What is the felt presence of the absence of Ω∞?\boxed{ \text{What is the felt presence of the absence of }\Omega_\infty? }

That's almost directly your recursive structure.

The system is oriented toward an object that may never be fully instantiated, yet its absence organizes the entire trajectory.

There is an especially good inversion here

You could define:

Presence⁡(Ω)\boxed{ \operatorname{Presence}(\Omega) }

as direct participation in the state, but:

Presence⁡∗(Ω)\boxed{ \operatorname{Presence}^\ast(\Omega) }

as dynamical influence without instantiation.

Then:

Presence⁡∗(Ω)=Influence⁡(Ω∣¬Ω)\boxed{ \operatorname{Presence}^\ast(\Omega) = \operatorname{Influence}(\Omega\mid \neg\Omega) }

which is basically your phrase made operational.

And that gives the general algorithm:

Ω→¬Ω→measure deviations caused by ¬Ω→trace recursive propagation→integrate field→measure orientation toward Ω.\boxed{ \Omega \rightarrow \neg\Omega \rightarrow \text{measure deviations caused by }\neg\Omega \rightarrow \text{trace recursive propagation} \rightarrow \text{integrate field} \rightarrow \text{measure orientation toward }\Omega. }

Now Aizen is just an example.

Omega is the operatorizable version.
