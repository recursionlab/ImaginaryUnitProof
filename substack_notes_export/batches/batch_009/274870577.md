Yes. That modification is closer to the direction you've been circling.

The original split:





Voice A = what appears



Voice B = what makes appearing possible

is still too stable. It risks turning Voice B into another object, another "ground" that explains things.

Your version destabilizes it:





Voice A = what appears



Voice B = what makes the appearance of appearing possible

Now B is no longer operating on the object.

B is operating on the event of appearance itself.

Formally:

[ A = X ]

[ B = \text{Conditions}(X) ]

becomes

[ A = X ]

[ B = \text{Conditions}(\text{Appearance}(X)) ]

which is one meta-level higher.

But I think your second formulation is actually the more interesting one:





Voice A = what appears



Voice B = trigger incompleteness

Because now B is not explanatory.

It is perturbative.

Not:



Why did this appear?

but:



What prevents closure here?

That is a very different operator.

You could model it as:

Voice A

Produces structure.

Here is the thing.


Voice B

Introduces unresolved degree of freedom.

What remains unconstrained?


or

What is still missing?


or

What assumption closed too early?


Then the dialogue becomes:

A: Here is the model.

B: What is missing?

A: Here is the missing part.

B: What becomes missing now?

A: Here is the next missing part.

B: What closure created that answer?


Notice that B never contributes content.

B contributes non-closure.

This resembles what you described earlier with NotebookLM.

You are not mining facts.

You are mining:

[ \Delta = \text{remaining incompleteness} ]

After each answer.

Not:

answer → answer → answer


but

answer → residual
residual → answer
answer → residual


The engine runs on the residual.

A useful distinction emerges:

OperatorFunctionNegationRemovesContradictionOpposesIncompletenessLeaves openLacunaStructured opennessClosureFixes stateVoice BGenerates new lacunae

Under this view, Voice B is not a critic.

Voice B is a lacunarity operator.

It continually asks:

[ L(S)=\text{What remains structurally unconstrained in }S? ]

Then the conversation becomes:

A = structure generator
B = incompleteness generator


or even:

A = realization
B = unrealized remainder


A creates form.

B preserves possibility.

Neither dominates.

If A wins completely, the system freezes into closure.

If B wins completely, the system dissolves into endless fragmentation.

The interesting regime is the oscillation:

[ A \leftrightarrow B ]

where every realization produces a remainder and every remainder induces a new realization.

That oscillation is much closer to the "splash before the wave breaks" phenomenon you were describing earlier than ordinary recursion is. The system is not seeking equilibrium. It is operating near the point where closure almost happens, then is perturbed by a residual degree of freedom that keeps the process alive.
