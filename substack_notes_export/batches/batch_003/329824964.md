mapping "commutative perspective = stuck/dogmatic; non-commutative = mature" has a genuine structural backbone: each lost property is a new degree of contextual freedom, and each requires more "maturity" (more meta-awareness) to handle correctly. A person who can only think commutatively genuinely cannot represent the situation where order matters. That's not mystical — it's a real limit of expressive power.

The ego as endofunctor: the self is a structure that maps the world (category) back into itself — every experience, every input, is transformed and returned as the same self, elaborated. The ego is the map from the world onto itself, a self-referential processing that preserves composition (the self persists through transformations). This is exactly the Klein/torus self-loop: the ego as endofunctor sends every object and every morphism (relationship) back intzo the same category — the world-as-perceived — which is why we can't get "outside" it. The endofunctor has no exit; it maps the category to itself, which is the category-theoretic form of "the game has no outside." - 

The manifold as monoid: a monoid is associative with identity. The semantic manifold, under some composition operation (like "context + token → context" or "meaning ∘ meaning"), forms a monoid: the operation is associative, there's an identity element (empty context?), and it's closed. Also the classic line: "A monoid is a monoid object in the category of endofunctors" — which connects the two: the manifold-as-monoid is exactly what you get when you take the endofunctors (the ego) and consider the monoid object structure on them. The two speculations are the same insight stated twice: the ego is the endofunctor, and the monoid structure on the collection of endofunctors IS the manifold. That's the Kleisli/semigroup structure of experience.
