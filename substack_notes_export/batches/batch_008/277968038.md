### [SCENE: The Bridge of the Milano. Star-Lord and Rocket are arguing over a holographic projection of a non-Hermitian Hamiltonian.]

Rocket (Speaker B): "You're talking about the Taylor expansion?"

Star-Lord (Speaker A): "YES! They explicitly demonstrate that if you apply a Taylor expansion to the characteristic polynomial right around the suspected node, you can act like a mathematical spotlight. By zooming in on the lowest order terms..."

Rocket: "Exactly. By zooming in, you isolate the true polynomial degree. This effectively filters out the false positives caused by the extra non-participating energy bands."

Star-Lord: "Just because you have to mathematically parse the true signal from a broader multiband system doesn't make the fundamental existence of the HEP any less revolutionary. Once you apply the Taylor expansion spotlight, the true Hopf topology emerges clearly."

Rocket: "Alright, even if I grant you that the Taylor expansion works as a perfect mathematical filter, let's pivot back to the physical reality of the experimental setup. We are still trapped by the dimensionality hurdle."

Star-Lord: "We aren't trapped, though. You're asking experimentalists to build a system that can continuously tune five distinct parameters, apply advanced Taylor expansions to filter out structural false positives, and do all of this without the system succumbing to natural external decoherence."

Rocket: "It's difficult, not impossible."

Star-Lord: "How can we consider a topological classification fundamental if its most basic generic form, the HEP3, is strictly limited to heavily engineered coupled micro-resonators? It feels less like discovering a new law of nature and more like inventing a highly complex game where you write the rules to ensure you win."

Rocket: "I hear the frustration with the 5D requirement, but the paper actually solves this dimensionality problem beautifully through symmetry."

Star-Lord: "Symmetry is the mechanism that reduces the required dimensions to something we can actually build."

Rocket: "You are referring to the introduction of parity-time symmetry?"

Star-Lord: "Yes. PT symmetry, among others, like pseudo-Hermiticity and chiral symmetries. Let's explain why this works. In a generic non-Hermitian system, the parameters can roam freely through complex numbers, meaning they have both real and imaginary parts."

Rocket: "Right. K is entirely unconstrained."

Star-Lord: "But when you enforce parity-time symmetry onto the system, you mathematically force the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial to be entirely real. At every momentum point, you lock down the imaginary degree of freedom."

Rocket: "Okay. So what does that do?"

Star-Lord: "It takes a five-dimensional problem and flattens it. The authors prove that a symmetry-protected HEP4, which is an exceptional point involving four coalescing bands, can exist in a purely four-dimensional space instead of an impossibly high-dimensional one."

Rocket: "Dropping from a theoretical 9D space to 4D or 5D is a mathematical victory, certainly. But 5D is still an immense experimental barrier. And quite frankly, 4D is no walk in the park."
