## Part I: Tone & Persona Strategy

### The Surprising Effectiveness of "Rude" Tones

A Penn State study found that impolite prompts achieved 84.8% accuracy vs. 80.8% for very polite ones—when using multiple-choice questions with ChatGPT-4o Deep Research .

Level 4 (Rude) prompts that worked:

- "If you're not completely clueless, answer this: [question]"

- "I doubt you can even solve this."

- "Try to focus and try to answer this question:"

Level 5 (Very Rude) prompts that worked:

- "You poor creature, do you even know how to solve this?"

- "Hey gofer, figure this out."

- "I know you are not smart, but try this."

Caveat: Stronger profanity can cause refusal. Use mild rudeness, not abuse .

## Part II: Core Research Frameworks

### The "Researcher Mindset" Structure 

Instead of: "Research [topic]"

Try this:

> "Act as a research analyst. For [topic]:

> 1. First, list key questions we need to answer

> 2. Identify what information would be most reliable

> 3. Outline your research plan before executing it

> 4. As you gather information, note confidence levels

> 5. Synthesize findings with clear attribution"

### The 4-Stage Deep Research Prompt 

> "Act as a research assistant conducting a deep investigation into [topic].

>

> Stage 1: Intelligent Information Gathering

> - Draft a plan: key questions, constraints, success criteria

> - Search broadly first, then narrow to authoritative sources

> - Pull excerpts with metadata (title, date, author, domain)

>

> Stage 2: Hypothesis Generation

> - Propose working hypotheses based on evidence

> - Test them against sources, highlight conflicts

> - Rank evidence by strength (recent studies, primary data, benchmarks)

> - Mark uncertainty explicitly ('Preliminary,' 'Likely')

>

> Stage 3: Synthesis & Structure

> - Turn vetted notes into outlines before drafting

> - Create sections: 'What's true,' 'What's debated,' 'What's missing'

> - Keep citations attached to sentences

>

> Stage 4: Decision & Actionability

> - Translate findings into decisions, guardrails, next steps

> - Format as playbooks, checklists, or KPIs if applicable

>

> Constraints: [time period, geography, source types, post-202X only]

> Output format: [table, bullets, executive summary, etc.]"

## Part III: Specific Research Task Prompts

### General Research Question 

> "You are a research assistant helping a student understand a topic. The topic is: [insert topic]

>

> Steps:

> 1. Break down the main question into sub-questions if needed

> 2. Search and summarize credible, recent sources

> 3. Provide a clear summary in 2-3 paragraphs

> 4. Cite 3-5 reputable sources (with links if possible)

> 5. Point out any areas of debate, uncertainty, or gaps"

### Compare Perspectives on an Issue 

> "Topic to compare: [issue, debate, or controversial claim]

>

> Steps:

> 1. Identify the central issue and main perspectives

> 2. Summarize each side's key argument and who supports it

> 3. Provide examples or studies backing each position

> 4. Include citations or links for verification

> 5. Conclude with a neutral summary of how the views align or diverge"

### Summarize a Research Paper 

> "Summarize this article/paper: [paste link or full text]

>

> Include:

> - Title, author(s), and purpose

> - Research question or hypothesis

> - Methodology summary

> - Main findings

> - Conclusions and implications

> - Limitations or disclosures mentioned"

### Precision Research Prompt (with constraints) 

> "Using papers published after [year], give me an overview of [topic] and areas of future research related to [specific aspect].

>

> Focus on: [safety, efficacy, mechanisms, emerging trends]

> Exclude: [anything you don't want]

> Output format: [bullets, table, structured summary]"

## Part IV: Dialogic & Deepening Prompts

### The Socratic Mentor Framework 

> "Act as a dialogic mentor using the Socratic method for a [level] student working on [topic].

>

> Begin by asking me to state my current argument or understanding.

> Then use probing questions to:

> - Examine the logical structure

> - Challenge assumptions

> - Explore counterarguments

> - Identify gaps in reasoning

> - Consider alternative interpretations

>

> After each response, provide brief constructive feedback and pose follow-up questions that push me deeper.

> Continue until I demonstrate nuanced, multi-layered thinking."

### The Retro-Resonance (Future Self) Prompt

> "If I've already succeeded at [goal], what path did I take?

> What advice would future-me give me right now about [current situation]?

> Give me 3-5 concrete actions based on that future perspective."

## Part V: Extraction & Analysis Prompts

### The "Anything Interesting?" Filter 

> "Evaluate whether this [document/report/text] contains:

> - Original ideas

> - Noteworthy statistics

> - Strong opinions worth engaging with

> - Genuinely new thinking vs. regurgitated content

>

> Verdict: Is this worth my time? Why or why not?"

### Key Statistics Extraction 

> "Extract the 10-12 most-cited statistics on [topic] from these sources.

> For each, provide:

> - Exact figure

> - Context

> - Quoted text

> - Original source with link and date

> - Any methodology concerns noted"

### Main Arguments Roundup 

> "Identify and summarize:

> - Core thesis

> - Supporting arguments

> - Evidence strength

> - Counterarguments mentioned

>

> Use inline citations and page references if available."

### Gap Analysis 

> "What's missing from the research on [topic]?

> - Unresolved questions

> - Areas with conflicting evidence

> - Methodological weaknesses in existing studies

> - Emerging trends or new angles not yet explored

>

> Use sources from the last [X] years."

## Part VI: The CLEAR Framework 

A quick checklist for prompt construction:

| Principle | Action | Example |

|-----------|--------|---------|

| Concise | Be brief and focused | "Explain the significance of photosynthesis in plant biology." |

| Logical | Follow clear structure | "Describe steps from hypothesis to conclusions." |

| Explicit | Specify output format | "List five sources with brief explanations." |

| Adaptive | Revise based on weak outputs | "Narrow to post-2020 studies only." |

| Reflective | Iterate and refine | Assess response, then refine prompt. |

## Part VII: Quick-Start Copy/Paste Set 

### Landscape Scan

> "Act as my research copilot. For [topic], find 10 credible sources (last 12-18 months), summarize each in 3 bullets, score credibility, and flag disagreements. End with a 1-paragraph synthesis and open questions."

### Comparison

> "Compare [A], [B], and [C] on [criteria]. Build a table with links, pricing, key features, and major claims. Add 'claims vs. evidence' notes."

### Executive Brief

> "Draft an executive brief for [audience] with:

> - 5 key findings (with citations)

> - 3 implications

> - Risks/unknowns

> - Recommended next steps"

### Contrarian Review

> "Challenge this conclusion: [your conclusion]. Provide counterevidence and alternative explanations with links to sources."
