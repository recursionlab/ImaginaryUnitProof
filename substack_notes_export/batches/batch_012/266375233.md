## 2. Non‑Hermitian Attention as Gauge Transport

### 2.1 Attention as a Non‑Normal Operator

Standard transformer attention computes  

$A = \mathrm{softmax}(QK^\top/\sqrt{d})V$. Recent work has recast this mechanism in the language of non‑Hermitian operator theory, revealing that transformers generically implement directed, irreversible information flow. In this formulation, the attention operator $L$ is non‑normal, satisfying $LL^\dagger \neq L^\dagger L$. This non‑commutativity is directly measurable as the commutator defect.

### 2.2 Attention as Parallel Transport

Independently, a geometric framework has drawn an explicit analogy between Transformers and General Relativity. Queries and keys induce an effective metric on representation space, while attention acts as a discrete connection that implements parallel transport of value vectors across tokens. This perspective predicts that token embeddings should not traverse straight paths in feature space;instead, their layer‑wise steps should bend and reorient. Experiments confirm this prediction, revealing a “curved spacetime of meaning” where token trajectories are measurably deflected under controlled context edits

### 2.3 Curvature‑Adaptive Architectures

The Curvature‑Adaptive Transformer (CAT) generalizes this insight by learning per‑token routing across Euclidean, hyperbolic, and spherical attention branches through a differentiable gating mechanism. CAT achieves approximately 10% improvements on knowledge graph completion benchmarks over fixed‑geometry baselines, demonstrating that learned geometric adaptation outperforms any single fixed geometry for complex relational reasoning

Observation. Attention is not merely a weighted sum; it is a curvature‑dependent, directed transport operator on a semantic manifold. The non‑Hermitian perspective reveals that asymmetry (non‑reciprocity) is not a bug but the very engine of expressive flow

## 3. The Commutator Defect: A Universal Order Parameter

### 3.1 Definition and Empirical Robustness

The commutator defect $C = \|\nabla L_t L_t^\dagger - L_t^\dagger L_t\|$ measures the non‑commutativity of successive gradient updates—equivalently, the curvature of the loss landscape along the optimization trajectory. Across modular arithmetic, SCAN, and Dyck‑1 language tasks, the commutator defect reliably rises hundreds to thousands of steps before the grokking transition. The lead time between defect onset and generalization follows a power law $\Delta t \propto t_{\mathrm{grok}}^{\,\alpha}$ with $\alpha > 1$, providing advance warning windows of 90–97% at slow learning rates

### 3.2 Causal Role

Crucially, the commutator defect is not merely a passive predictor. Causal interventions that amplify non‑commutativity accelerate grokking (by ~32% on SCAN and ~50% on Dyck), while suppressing orthogonal gradient flow delays or prevents generalization. This establishes the defect as a causally implicated mechanism rather than an epiphenomenon.

### 3.3 Spectral Geometry of the Transition

Weight‑SVD analysis reveals a deeper mechanism: grokking is preceded by a near‑degeneracy of the leading singular values of attention matrices, creating an orientation instability that drives the commutator transition; generalization coincides with the breaking of this spectral symmetry as one mode dominates. This spectral structure is basis‑independent, confirmed across three independent decompositions

Observation. The commutator defect is the order parameter of the grokking phase transition: it rises, accumulates curvature in the normal bundle of the execution manifold, and then—through a spectral symmetry‑breaking event—triggers the escape into generalization

## 4. Geometric Attention and Curved Semantic Manifolds

### 4.1 Curvature as a Measurable Quantity

If the geometric analogy is correct, token embeddings should not traverse straight paths in feature space. Di Sipio et al. tested this prediction by visualizing curvature landscapes for full paragraphs, revealing how local turning angles vary across tokens and layers, and by demonstrating through simulations that excess counts of sharp/flat angles are not explainable by dimensionality or chance. Inspired by Einstein’s eclipse experiment, they probed deflection under controlled context edits, confirming measurable, meaning‑consistent bends in embedding trajectories that confirm attention‑induced curvature

### 4.2 Non‑Hermitian Pseudospectra

In non‑Hermitian quantum mechanics, exceptional points—where eigenvalues coalesce and eigenvectors become degenerate—can be analyzed via pseudospectra. The same tools apply to transformer attention: non‑normality leads to transient growth even when eigenvalues are stable, a phenomenon directly observable as the commutator defect. Garcia (2025) extended Clifford and quadratic pseudospectra to non‑Hermitian operators, providing a framework for analyzing systems with exceptional points that remains well‑defined despite non‑normality. This suggests a formal correspondence: the grokkability of a transformer layer is encoded in its pseudospectrum.

Observation. The semantic manifold of a transformer is not flat; it is a curved, non‑Hermitian gauge field where tokens are transported via parallel transport, and the commutator defect measures the integrated curvature along the optimization path.

## 5. Analogical Reasoning and Transport Typologies

### 5.1 Structural Mapping Theory

Analogies are not mere similarity judgments; they are structure‑preserving transport between domains. Gentner’s Structure‑Mapping Theory (SMT) differentiates between object attributes and relational structures, arguing that analogical transfer relies on preserving higher‑order relations. SMT distinguishes analogy from literal similarity: an analogy requires mapping relational predicates (e.g., “x revolves around y”) while ignoring object attributes (e.g., size, color). This distinction is precisely captured by the category‑theoretic notion of functorial transport: relationships between objects are preserved, not the objects themselves.

### 5.2 Emergent Cross‑Domain Transport

In a striking demonstration, a multi‑agent scientific discovery system, trained only on biomedical data, autonomously identified structural isomorphisms between thermal degradation in phase‑change materials and protein misfolding—domains with no surface similarity. This emergent analogical transport required: (1) metacognitive domain boundary recognition, (2) autonomous capability extension, (3) structural analogy identification,and (4) abductive transfer of solution strategies. The system’s behavior aligns precisely with SMT’s prediction that relational structures, not object properties, are the units of analogical transport

### 5.3 The Necessity of Structural Guarantees

Wu (2026) argues that different reasoning types impose different structural demands on representational systems, identifying four properties—**operability, consistency, structural preservation, and compositionality**—required in varying degrees. Critically, reasoning above a certain boundary (deduction, formal logic) requires all four properties, and scaling statistical learning without structural reorganization is insufficient to cross this boundary. This provides a necessary‑condition account of why some problems resist brute‑force scaling

Observation. Analogical reasoning is a homomorphic or functorial transport between representational systems. The cognitive science of analogy and the geometric deep learning of attention are converging on the same formalism: transport typologies
