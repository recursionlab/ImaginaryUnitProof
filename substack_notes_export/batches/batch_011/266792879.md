## The AST Lift Pipeline

The core operational flow is:

\[

\mathrm{AST} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathrm{Chart} \xrightarrow{\text{spectral}} \mathrm{Singularity} \xrightarrow{\text{Resolution}} \mathrm{GlobalSection} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Rec}\mu} \text{stable fixed point}.

\]

- AST → Chart: Every abstract syntax tree (or any descriptive unit) is transported (\(\Delta\)) into a local chart – a self‑consistent semantic fragment.

- Chart → Singularity: Charts are scanned by a spectral singularity detector (based on graph Laplacian \(L = D - A\) and instability of eigenvalues). If a chart’s instability exceeds a threshold, it is flagged as singular.

- Singularity → Resolution: Singularities are repaired using coercion/repair (\(\mathcal C\)) and the collapse residue \(\⦳\).

- Resolution → GlobalSection: Repaired charts are glued (\(\mathcal G\)) into a global, consistent section.

- GlobalSection → stable fixed point: The entire loop is iterated under \(\mathrm{Rec}\mu\) until three termination conditions hold:

  1. \(\mathcal F_{\Delta,\⦳} \to 0\) (a measure of inconsistency vanishes),

  2. Hash of the repo stops changing,

  3. All “hard gates” (invariants) pass.

Thus the pipeline turns static descriptions into a dynamical, self‑correcting manifold.

## Key Invariants and Geometries

Three “hard gates” enforce correctness:

- Retention invariant: \(R(T) = \frac{|\mathcal I(X) \cap \mathcal I(T(X))|}{|\mathcal I(X)|}\) measures how many invariants of \(X\) survive after transformation \(T\). Must stay high.

- Transport‑collapse commutator: \([\Delta,\⦳](x) := \Delta(\⦳(x)) - \⦳(\Delta(x)) \in \mathcal D\) (the defect space). Non‑zero indicates inconsistency between transport and residue extraction.

- Cocycle condition: \(g_{ij} g_{jk} g_{ki} = 1\) on triple overlaps – standard sheaf/gluing condition.

Defect geometry is modelled by \(\⦳\), which is a linear idempotent that commutes with the action (\(a \star \⦳(x) = \⦳(a \star x)\)). The associator \(\tau(a,b,c) = (a \star b) \star c - a \star (b \star c)\) measures non‑associativity; it lives in the defect space \(\mathcal D\). This prepares the ground for the Cayley‑Dickson 32D defect scalar – a global measure of non‑associativity (like the norm of the associator over the 32‑dimensional basis). This is a powerful way to quantify “algebraic defect” in a unified manner.

## Repair Dynamics

The system evolves according to two intertwined dynamics:

- Discrete: \(x_{t+1} = \Delta(x_t) + \Gamma(\⦳(x_t))\) – transport the current state, then add a repair term \(\Gamma\) that depends on the residue.

- Continuous (Langevin‑type): \(\dot x_t = -\nabla \Upsilon(x_t, R(x_t)) + \xi_t\) – gradient descent on a forgetting/saturation potential \(\Upsilon(\hat a,\hat b) = 1/(|\hat a \star \hat b| + \varepsilon)\), with noise \(\xi_t\). The potential becomes large when the “product” of two objects is small, i.e., when they are almost incompatible – this drives them apart or forces a repair.

The saturation factor \(F_c(t) = \exp\!\left(-\int_0^t \Upsilon(x_\tau, R(x_\tau)) d\tau\right)\) measures how much the system has “forgotten” past conflicts – it decays when conflicts are high, forcing re‑evaluation.

## Termination and Fixed Point

The recursion \(\mathrm{Rec}\mu(\mathcal R) = \mathrm{Fix}\bigl(\mathsf{Glue} \circ \mathsf{Inf} \circ \Delta \circ \mathsf{Repair}\bigr)\) says: apply repair, then transport, then interpret (give semantics), then glue; find a fixed point of this composite. This is a higher‑order, non‑linear fixed point – essentially a self‑consistent global state that no longer changes under the repair loop.

The termination conditions ensure:

- No residual inconsistency (\(\mathcal F_{\Delta,\⦳} \to 0\)),

- No syntactic change (hash stability),

- All invariants hold (gates pass).

Thus the system has operationally closed.
