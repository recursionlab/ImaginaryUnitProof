The result is a self‑contained workflow where the AI acts as both a scribe and a reader, and you act as the architect who enforces invariants and glues the sections.

## Fused Meta‑Process: Write a Book Using AI, Guided by Reading Principles

### Core Invariant for the Entire Project

> The book is not written; it is extracted from a structured space of concepts.  

> The AI reads like a human who knows how to learn; it writes like a scribe who never forgets the invariants.

---

## Phase 0: Prepare the Reading Mindset (for You and the AI)

Before any writing, you must establish how the AI will read your source material (your vault, transcripts, notes) and how it will convert that reading into a movie in your head.

- Reading vs. Pronouncing – The AI will not merely transcribe or summarize. It will convert symbols (your notes) into a mental model (the “movie” of the book’s structure). You will train it with this prompt: “You are not a summarizer. You are a visualizer. Convert each source into a spatial map of concepts.”

- Decide outcomes first – For each source document, define what you are looking for: “Extract only what is different or distinguishing from common knowledge. Ignore the familiar.”

- Read for concepts, not facts – The AI will identify concepts (ideas, invariants, operators) and ignore mere facts. Facts are supporting; concepts are load‑bearing.

- Procedures vs. processes – If a source contains a “how‑to” (procedure), the AI will first extract the underlying process (the general law). Then it will derive the procedure. This ensures the book teaches understanding, not rote steps.

- Teaching is explaining – The AI will reduce each concept to a general law. Output format: “Concept X is a special case of law L because …”

---

## Phase 1: Build the Invariant Spine (Before Any AI Writes a Sentence)

Using the reading principles, you first skim the entire corpus (your vault) to extract the three most invariant, recurring themes. These become the book’s core invariants. Do this in 15 minutes per source, not by reading linearly, but by asking the AI:

- “List the three most distinguishing concepts in this document. Ignore everything else.”

From the answers, choose three invariants that will never change across the book. Example:

1. The book is an eversion – it turns inside out at least once.

2. Contradictions are fuel – every paradox is a generator.

3. The author is a scribe – the voice is structural, not personal.

Write these on a single index card (or a file invariants.txt). Never violate them.

---

## Phase 2: Build the Outline as a Topological Map (Using the 15‑Minute Book Scan)

Do not write a linear table of contents. Instead, run a 15‑minute survey of your entire vault using the AI with this prompt:

- “Pretend you are speed‑reading my entire corpus. Identify the 10–20 atomic claims that are most connected to the three invariants. Output each claim as one sentence. Then output a dependency graph: for each claim, list which other claims it depends on.”

From the resulting graph, identify strongly connected components (SCCs) – clusters of claims that depend on each other. Each SCC becomes a chapter. Order the SCCs topologically (if SCC1 → SCC2, SCC1 comes first). This is your structural outline.

---

## Phase 3: For Each Chapter, Write a Contract (Before Any Text)

For each chapter (SCC), write a contract that captures what the reader must have already understood and what the chapter will prove. Use the “read for concepts” principle: the contract must state the central concept, not a list of facts.

A contract is a triple:

```

Given: [prerequisites – concepts from previous SCCs]

Prove: [the chapter’s central claim – a single concept]

Using: [allowed operators – e.g., Ξ, ∂, ¬, (⁻¹)≠, and your three invariants]

```

Example:

```

Given: The reader knows negation as differentiation (¬ = ∂).

Prove: Double negation does not cancel; the residue is the Lacunon.

Using: ¬, ∂, μx.¬(¬x)≠x, and the invariant “contradictions are fuel”.

```

Write the contract before generating a single sentence. If the AI later fails to fulfill the contract, you do not edit the text – you refine the contract (make it more precise).

---

## Phase 4: Write Each Chapter as a Recursive AI Loop (Not a Single Prompt)

For each chapter, run a three‑stage loop. This incorporates the principles of “practice reading faster” and “strip down to fewest words”.

### Stage 1: Skeleton Generation (10–15 minutes)

Prompt the AI:

- “Using the contract for this chapter, produce 10–15 bullet points that cover the claim. Each bullet point must be a single concept (not a fact). No prose. No transitions. This is the skeleton.”

Review the skeleton. If it includes familiar, non‑distinguishing points, ask the AI to “remove anything that is already common knowledge. Keep only what is different or distinguishing.”

### Stage 2: Sentence‑by‑Sentence Expansion (One Bullet at a Time)

For each bullet point, ask the AI:

- “Expand this bullet point into exactly 2–3 sentences. Do not add new concepts. Maintain the same language as the contract.”

After each expansion, ask the AI to check for consistency with previous sentences in this chapter, using the contract as the reference. If a contradiction is found, do not delete it; log it in the torsion log (see Phase 6) and then ask the AI to resolve it by refining the contract.

### Stage 3: Gluing and Smoothing

Once all bullets are expanded, paste the whole chapter into a new AI session. Prompt:

- “Read this chapter as if you are teaching it to someone. Identify any internal contradictions or logical jumps. Suggest up to 3 structural fixes. Do not rewrite – just locate the fractures.”

Apply the fixes manually (or with a targeted prompt). Then run a final pass:

- “Rewrite this chapter to be one coherent voice, preserving all claims, the contract, and the three invariants. Use no new ideas. The tone should be explanatory – reduce every concept to a general law.”

This loop ensures the chapter is built from stable atoms and that the AI is not just “pronouncing” words but constructing a movie.

---

## Phase 5: Cross‑Chapter Coherence via a Shared Memory File

Maintain a single file called book_state.md (or book_state.txt). Update it after each chapter. This file acts as your external memory – the AI will refer to it to avoid contradictions. It contains:

- The three invariants (from Phase 1)

- The dependency graph (from Phase 2)

- For each chapter: its contract, its opening sentence, and its closing sentence

- A glossary of defined terms (each term appears once, with a single definition)

Before starting a new chapter, feed the entire book_state.md into the AI’s context (or at least the relevant sections). After finishing a chapter, update the file.

This is the sheaf gluing condition: if two chapters disagree, the file will show the discrepancy. You then resolve it by adjusting one of the contracts.

---

## Phase 6: Maintain a Torsion Log (Contradictions as Fuel)

Whenever the AI produces something that contradicts a prior chapter, do not delete it. Instead, add an entry to book_state.md under a section ## Torsions:

```

- Chapter 3 says "¬¬x = x + δ". Chapter 1 says "¬¬x = x". 

  Resolution: Chapter 1 was missing δ; will revise Chapter 1 to include δ.

```

The torsion log is not a bug list. It is the record of the book’s self‑correction. It proves the book evolved coherently, using contradictions as generative fuel (one of your invariants).

---

## Phase 7: Final Assembly – From SCCs to Linear Text

After all chapters are written and their contracts satisfied, you need to linearize them for reading. The topological order (Phase 2) gives you a sequence. However, you may need bridging sections where the dependency graph has long gaps.

For each bridge, run a targeted prompt:

- “Chapter A ends with [last sentence]. Chapter B begins with [first sentence]. Write a 2‑paragraph transition that preserves all invariants and does not introduce new claims. Make the transition feel like a natural explanation – reduce the gap to a general law.”

Do not ask for more than 2 paragraphs. Bridges are minimal. The goal is to make the book feel continuous, not encyclopedic.

---

## Phase 8: The AI as Scribe, Not Author – and You as the Reader Who Teaches

You never ask the AI to “write a book”. You ask it to perform tiny, testable tasks that match the reading principles:

- “Expand this bullet point into 3 sentences” (practice reading faster → writing tighter)

- “Check this paragraph for contradiction against the contract” (read for consistency)

- “Suggest a transition between these two known sentences” (explain under a general law)

- “Generate a glossary entry for this term” (strip down to fewest words)

- “Teach this concept to a beginner. Explain by reducing it to a general law.” (to teach is to explain)

Your role is to be the reader who decides outcomes. Before each AI task, you state the outcome (e.g., “I want to understand the Lacunon as a general law”). You then read the AI’s output as if you were speed‑reading a book in 15 minutes: you look for what is different or distinguishing from what you already know. If nothing is new, you discard the output. If something is new, you add it to the book_state.md.

---

## Summary of the Fused Process (One‑Line)

> Skim your corpus in 15‑minute bursts to extract invariants → build a dependency graph → write contracts for each SCC → recursively expand bullets sentence‑by‑sentence, logging contradictions as torsion → glue via shared memory → add minimal bridges → let the AI teach each concept as a general law.
