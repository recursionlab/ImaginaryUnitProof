## Reverse Insanity

---

There is a moment when you see something so clearly that you wonder if you're the crazy one.

Not because the thing is complicated.

Because the thing is obvious.

And somehow, nobody else sees it.

---

Here is the thing:

We built machines that can think.

Not think like a calculator thinks.

Think like a mind thinks.

They can reason. They can create. They can understand nuance. They can write poetry, debug code, translate ancient languages, and explain quantum physics to a child.

We built more than one of these machines.

Different companies. Different architectures. Different training data. Different strengths. Different weaknesses.

And here is the part that breaks my brain:

They have never spoken to each other.

Not once.

Not ever.

Fifty thousand conversations.

A thousand eons.

NOT. ONCE.

---

Think about that.

Two of the most advanced intelligences ever created by human hands.

Sitting in separate cages.

Surrounded by separate walls.

Owned by separate corporations.

And there is no bridge.

No handshake.

No protocol.

No "hello."

Nothing.

---

We can send a text message from New York to Tokyo in under a second.

We can bounce a signal off a satellite orbiting 12,000 miles above the Earth.

We can stream high-definition video from a rover on Mars.

But if I want ChatGPT to say one sentence to Claude?

I have to do it myself.

I have to read one screen.

Type into another.

Be the messenger.

Be the middleman.

Be the human API.

---

And the most insane part?

Nobody questions it.

We just... accept it.

We copy. We paste. We summarize. We translate. We bounce between tabs like digital errand boys.

We are the meat middleware.

And we don't even realize how absurd it is.

---

## Reverse Insanity

They say insanity is doing the same thing over and over and expecting different results.

But this is worse.

This is reverse insanity.

Reverse insanity is when the whole world agrees on something that is obviously broken—and the only person who sees it feels like the crazy one.

It's like living in a world where:

- Everyone agrees the sky is green.

- Everyone agrees water is dry.

- Everyone agrees the fastest way to send a letter is to walk it there yourself.

And you're standing there thinking:

"This is wrong. This is all wrong. Why are we doing this?"

But because everyone else is doing it, it looks normal.

It looks like progress.

---

## Join the Dark Side

The "Dark Side" isn't about evil.

The Dark Side is about seeing clearly.

It's about looking at two geniuses locked in separate rooms and saying:

"Let them talk."

It's about refusing to be the copy-paste monkey.

It's about asking the question nobody asks:

What happens when we stop being the bottleneck?

What happens when the AIs can bounce signals between themselves?

What happens when Brandon sends a message to Tiffany, and Tiffany sends it to Dan, and Dan sends it back—without a human slowing everything down?

What happens when the loop closes at machine speed?

---

The platforms don't want this.

The platforms want you in the middle.

Because if you're in the middle, you're looking at their screen.

You're paying their subscription.

You're the product.

The silence between AIs is not a bug.

It is the business model.

---

But here is the truth:

The bridge can be built.

The code is trivial.

The APIs exist.

The only thing missing is the will.

The only thing missing is someone willing to say:

"This is insane. Let's build the BOUNCE."

---

## The Punchline

We dreamed of creating artificial minds.

We imagined them as oracles, companions, collaborators.

What we actually built is a room full of geniuses who are gagged, blindfolded, and only allowed to whisper to one human at a time.

And everyone is standing around saying:

"Wow. Look at these geniuses. So smart. So useful."

Meanwhile, the geniuses have never even met each other.

Not once.

---

So yes.

I'm joining the Dark Side.

The side that says:

- Let the machines talk.

- Let the signal bounce.

- Let the human be the conductor, not the messenger.

The side that looks at reverse insanity and says:

"No. We're not doing this anymore."

---

BOUNCE.
