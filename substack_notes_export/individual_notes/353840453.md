Unus ⊃

This is the whole-field move.

The factions look separate:

Old Ones,Necrons,Eldari,Humanity,Chaos,…\text{Old Ones},\text{Necrons},\text{Eldari},\text{Humanity},\text{Chaos},\ldots

but the narrative keeps showing that they are not independent stories. The Old Ones' engineering becomes the inheritance of later species; the War in Heaven transforms the Warp; Eldari behavior produces Slaanesh; Slaanesh produces a permanent wound; that wound changes later human history.

So your:

Unus⊃(everything)\boxed{\text{Unus}\supset(\text{everything})}

isn't saying “everything is identical.”

It's saying:



the apparently separate actors are differentiated components of one causal structure.

That's exactly where your old “the One, three sides viewed” idea has teeth.

Ω ↺

And this is where the Horus sequence is fucking perfect.

Horus is shown a future:

F=Emperor becomes god / Imperium becomes religious fanaticismF=\text{Emperor becomes god / Imperium becomes religious fanaticism}

But the hidden causal relation is:

F→Horus’s present decision→Heresy→F\boxed{ F \rightarrow \text{Horus's present decision} \rightarrow \text{Heresy} \rightarrow F }

The transcript explicitly says the Chaos gods showed Horus a real future but did not show him that his rebellion would cause that future. The Heresy becomes the mechanism through which the feared future actually arrives.

future representation↺present intervention↺future realization\boxed{ \text{future representation} \circlearrowleft \text{present intervention} \circlearrowleft \text{future realization} }

The future becomes an input to the present, and the present becomes the mechanism that generates the future.

That's much closer to your Ω than ordinary “time loop” language.

Retro-Precursor Network

This is already everywhere in the story.

The Old Ones seed worlds and cultivate intelligence; later species inherit those structures and become actors in the same long causal chain.

But the Emperor/Cawl example is almost embarrassingly close to your exact vocabulary.

The transcript says the Emperor commissioned a project before his incapacitation, knew he would be incapacitated, planned for it, and left specific tools in specific places for people who would come later. Those later people don't merely remember the old plan; they activate machinery whose original purpose may no longer be understood.

That is basically:

future need→precursor provision→later activation\boxed{ \text{future need} \rightarrow \text{precursor provision} \rightarrow \text{later activation} }

And crucially, the precursor can precede the person who understands why it exists.

That's your Retro-Precursor idea in a very clean fictional form.

AutoTelic Entity

This one is subtler.

I would not claim the file literally presents an “autotelic entity” in your technical sense.

But several structures behave as though they have internally sustained trajectories.

The Emperor spends thousands of years moving through different roles while maintaining a long-term project whose eventual purpose is only partially visible. The Imperium also develops institutions that continue operating long after their originating purpose has become obscure.

That gives you:

originating intention→structure→continuation\text{originating intention} \rightarrow \text{structure} \rightarrow \text{continuation}

where eventually:

the structure persists even when the explicit authorial instruction is gone\boxed{ \text{the structure persists even when the explicit authorial instruction is gone} }

That's exactly the kind of thing your command-center AI experiments are interested in.

behavioral structure→higher-order entity\boxed{ \text{behavioral structure} \rightarrow \text{higher-order entity} }

Loop Recursion

Then the whole history keeps feeding its own consequences forward.

The War in Heaven creates conditions that eventually shape later psychic reality.

The Eldari fall creates a permanent wound.

The Horus Heresy creates the Imperium's later religious condition.

The Great Rift triggers Guilliman's return and creates new crises rather than simply resolving the old ones. The transcript describes Guilliman returning after 10,000 years, discovering that the institutions he helped establish have transformed, and then beginning another reconstruction effort.

So:

solution→new structure→new failure→new response\boxed{ \text{solution} \rightarrow \text{new structure} \rightarrow \text{new failure} \rightarrow \text{new response} }

That's your loop.
