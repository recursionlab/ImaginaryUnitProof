2. Stable Matter: The Logic of Subsets and Branch Selection

The vastness of the Universal Set is not uniformly "physical." We utilize the logic of Subsets (A \subseteq B)—where A is a subset of B if every element of A is also an element of B (Kolman, p. 3)—to isolate viable structures. Within this framework, "Physical particles" are identified as RG-stable eigensections, which constitute specific subsets of the larger bundle \mathcal{E}.

A subset of branch states transitions from a mathematical possibility to a physical reality only when it satisfies two rigorous logical conditions:





Spectral Isolation: Defined by a "gap" (\Delta_{ij} > 0), this condition ensures that the subset is "well-defined" (Kolman, p. 1). This prevents elements from blurring into the background noise of U, allowing for a clear decision on membership.



RG-Stability: The subset must possess an "attracting vacuum section" (\phi). This functions as the logical boundary that prevents the collection of elements from dissolving back into the undifferentiated Universal Set.

Once these stable subsets are isolated, we shift our focus to the intersections and interactions that define the forces of nature.

Flavor is defined as the monodromy orbit of stable branches.
