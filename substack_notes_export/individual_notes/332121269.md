Engineering is a form of trusted advising. He(Andrew Ng) repeatedly used this framing:

> "Get yourself into the mindset of being a trusted adviser of the company that you're interviewing for." "Nobody can understand the implications of generated code better than an engineer."

This means a well-engineered software professional isn't someone who writes the most code — they're someone who understands the business, the risks, the people, and the long-term consequences of what gets built.

He also explicitly linked engineering quality to career survival:

> "The bottom line is the bottom line." "Business focus is non-negotiable." "Responsibility has evolved from fluffy social issues to hard business concerns like protecting the company's reputation."

---

## In One Sentence

> Well-engineered software, to Lawrence Moroney, is software that intentionally delivers business value through human-understandable code, managed with strategic awareness of its long-term costs — not software that's merely generated, but software that's thoughtfully built with clear intent, planning, and reflection.
