\]

### Interpretation:

- Let \( |⧉\rangle \) be a normalized quantum state, so \( \langle ⧉ | ⧉ \rangle = 1 \).

- Let \( A \) be a Hermitian operator representing an observable.

- The inner product \( \langle ⧉ | A ⧉ \rangle \) is the expectation value of \( A \) in the state \( |⧉\rangle \), denoted \( \langle A \rangle \).

- With normalization, the entire expression simplifies to \( \langle A \rangle \), as shown below:

Let:

- \( a = \langle ⧉ | A ⧉ \rangle \) (expectation value of A),

- \( b = \langle ⧉ | ⧉ \rangle = 1 \) (since normalized).

Then:

- The left part: \( \langle a | b \rangle = a^* b = a^* \) (since b=1).

- The right part: \( \langle b | b \rangle = |b|^2 = 1 \).

- The outer product: \( \langle a^* | 1 \rangle = (a^*)^* \cdot 1 = a = \langle A \rangle \).

Thus, the expression evaluates to \( \langle A \rangle \), a fundamental quantity in quantum mechanics that represents the average value of a measurement of \( A \) on the state \( |⧉\rangle \)
