Execution rule

No nonstandard operator, predicate, transform, relation, or overloaded notation executes before declaration. Standard mathematical operators retain conventional meaning unless overridden below. Parameterized declarations apply to every indexed instance they name.

Precedence:

P₀: parentheses / explicit grouping

P₁: indices, subscripts, function application, semantic pairing ⟨A∣B⟩

P₂: composition ∘ and inner composition ∘₍ᵢₙ₎

P₃: unary transforms ∇, ¬, ⊘, Θ, Trace, Class, Closure, Inv, Req, Capacity

P₄: transition operators →ᶠ, ↝, ⇄

P₅: comparison relations =, ≠, ≃, ≋, ⇏, ∈, ⊆

P₆: conjunction ∧

P₇: disjunction ∨

P₈: implication ⇒

P₉: declaration assignment :=

Parentheses override all precedence.

A subscript/index binds before the operator it modifies.

An explicit semicolon ; separates operator arguments and does not form a logical relation.

A transition modifier binds to the immediately preceding source and following target: A→ρB means ρ(A)=B.

A compound transition is evaluated left-to-right unless parentheses or a declared bidirectional operator override that order.

A⇄B is one structural relation; it does not expand automatically into two independently executable transitions.

A↝B carries admissibility semantics only; it does not inherit equality, determinism, or reversibility.

Logical expressions are evaluated only after all enclosed operator applications and structural relations have been resolved.
